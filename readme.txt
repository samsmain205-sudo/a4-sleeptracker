6. Did you design your app with a particular type of user in mind? If so, whom?
  Yes, I aimed for a younger audience: specifically female college students. Hence, the app is cuter and has more feminine color usage, as I wanted to make an app I would use! 


7. Did you design your app specifically for iOS or Android, or both?
  Both, since it used basic features such as a bottom navigation bar with labeling. As well as simple ways to log and view information, keeping the design of the app simple and intuitive.


8. How can a person log overnight sleep in your app? Why did you choose to support logging overnight sleep in this way?
  There are two parts to the user input: one when the user went to bed and when they woke up on the home page labeled Log Sleep. I chose this way essentially to allow users to log sleep anytime since my target audience is college students; they may forget to log data.  


9. How can a person log sleepiness during the day in your app? Why did you choose to support logging sleepiness in this way?
  Through the Ionics range component, users can drag the knob from a scale of 1-7 based on the Stanford sleepiness scale when clicking log mood in the bottom nav-bar. I chose this since I found it intuitive to drag a scale to display the number value you want while having a button on the bottom to confirm that's the one the user wants.


10. How can a person view the data they logged in your app? Why did you choose to support viewing logged data in this way?
  They click View Data, and there's a list that shows all logged data. I chose this way so users don't have to search for it and can just click View Data to easily see the list of all data logged.


11. Which feature choose--using a native device resource, backing up logged data, or both?
  I chose native device resources by using local notifications from Capacitor.  


12. If you used a native device resource, what feature did you add? How does this feature change the app's experience for a user?
  Used local notifications to remind users to log their sleepiness after logging their sleep, and vice versa. Changes the user experience to remind them to log data and become consistent in logging both.
